PANORAMA

Stitched view

This runs the full offline stitcher — multi-scale features, guided matching, a bundle adjustment over every camera angle and the shared focal length, outlier pruning, a second solve, and seam-cut compositing — as Python under WebAssembly. It is the same tools/stitch_lab.py a desktop rebuild runs, over the same archive, so the two produce the same panorama. The runtime is about 25 MB the first time and is then cached; no capture data is uploaded.

The headline number is overlap disagreement — how far two photographs differ about the same direction, per colour channel. It is used instead of the solver's own residual because the residual is computed after the disagreeing matches have been pruned away, so it improves when evidence is discarded. On a measured two-lap capture the residual called the build very slightly better while the picture was 36% worse.

The camera and sensors are paused. You can put the phone down — this runs on its own.

An equirectangular panorama is a map projection, so a straight roofline above the horizon is drawn as a curve and the flat picture cannot tell you whether bowing is the projection or a real error. Here a straight edge in the world is straight on screen. Drag to look around; scroll to zoom. If a line snaps straight, the stitch was right and the flattening was lying.

Build this after a survey to see the profile drawn over the imagery it came from. Scroll sideways; the ruler is azimuth. Turn off Blend seams to see each frame cut hard against its neighbour — uglier, but a step in a roofline is then a measurable geometry error rather than something smoothed away.

TRUE BEARING CHECK

Tap a landmark you can identify in the stitched view — a chimney, a pole, a distinct treetop — then type its true bearing taken from a map. Everything else on this page is self-consistent: the imagery is painted at the azimuth the app believes, so an azimuth that is uniformly offset or slowly drifting produces a picture that looks perfectly correct while pointing the wrong way. This is the only check here that reaches outside the survey.

Compositing decides what a geometry error looks like. Seam cut gives each region to one photograph and cuts where they already agree, which is right for a finished picture. Nearest axis takes every pixel from the single frame that saw it closest to its optical axis, so a misregistration becomes a hard step whose size you can read off the azimuth scale — uglier, and the honest choice when you are diagnosing rather than presenting. Feather averages the overlap, which turns the same error into a smooth blur that looks correct and is not.

EVIDENCE

Coverage detail

A bin is verified when it has independent evidence — either two passes, or enough tight, confident looks gathered inside a single lap. The single-lap route exists because a second lap is walked from a slightly different standing position, and against anything close that is real parallax the stitcher cannot undo. Measured on a two-lap capture, frames from different laps disagreed 2.4× more than frames from the same lap, and 6.6× more at the 90th percentile. Getting a bearing finished on the first lap is worth more than confirming it on a second.

A frame is omitted when no verified visual overlap connects it to the rest of the solution. Painting it anyway from its sensor pose would place an unchecked island in the middle of a solved panorama, which is worse than a gap, so the stitcher leaves it out and says so here. “Moved” is how far the solve rotated that frame away from where the sensors put it.
