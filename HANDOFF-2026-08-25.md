# Horizon Scanner — v0.21.0, 2026-08-25

Written from the back-yard capture of the same date: a Pixel, 115 photographs,
157 seconds of walking, 980 seconds of building. Everything below is measured
from the archives in that capture's debug ZIP and is reproducible with the tools
named at the end.

The panorama from that capture was good over most of the ring and had one large
gap and mismatch. The guidance dot was, in the operator's words, "so broken it's
worse than useless".

---

## What the dot was actually doing

It was a mirror of the phone. Both axes.

**Horizontally**, `ScanGuidance.update()` held a column by setting the target to
`plan.bearingOf(plan.indexOf(headingDeg))` — the operator's *own* bearing,
rounded to a 2° bin. It could not lead; it could only shadow. The recorded trail
shows the target walking 255 → 253 → 251 → 249 behind a phone that was choosing
its own path, at an offset of a few tenths of a degree.

**Vertically**, the dot climbed only when `coverage.needsLiftAt()` said a top had
never been framed, descended only well above a sector's skyline, and *otherwise
rode at the camera's own elevation*. During a column hold that third case is the
one that applied nearly always — the top had been seen, so no lift was wanted —
so the dot sat at the elevation the camera already held. The trail has
six-second stretches of camera at 6.8°, dot at 6.8°, required 15.2°, lift 0.0,
while the band at 14.9° that the column needed quietly failed to fill.

Measured over the 533 recorded samples:

| | field | after this change |
|---|---|---|
| dot within 2° of the operator's own pose | **80%** | 20% |
| vertical ask that merely mirrored the camera | **77%** | 11% |
| vertical ask that named a specific plan band | 0% | 65% |

## The deadlock underneath it

The two maps had different ceilings and nobody had noticed.

`CoverageMap` stops asking for elevation at `maxRequestedElevationDeg` (60°) and
marks anything taller `beyondTilt`, so the ring never blocks on a top it cannot
reach. `ColumnPlan` had no ceiling at all. The house measured **75.1°**, so
`requireHeight` demanded six bands, whose top band centre is **74.4°** — a
height nothing in the app will ever ask the operator to aim at.

So 19 columns could never complete, `columnBusy` was permanently true on them,
and the hold could only ever end by timing out. **60 of the 180 bins** finished
that capture flagged `beyondTilt`: a third of the ring in a state the ring had
forgiven and the column plan had not.

That is the threshold the operator said was never reached. It was not reachable.

## What else was wrong in the same file

- **Progress was measured by `lowestGap` alone.** Filling band 3 while band 1
  was still open registered as no progress, so the patience timer ran anyway.
  Now `bandsFilled()` counts every filled band.
- **`waitingSec` asked whether the TARGET had moved.** While the dot was pinned
  to the heading it moved constantly, so the timer never accumulated, the
  six-second escape never fired, and the interface never once said "hold here".
  It now measures whether the ground *under the dot* gained anything — which is
  the local question, and the only one that means anything.
- **`nextTarget()` was dead code**, and it flipped `plan.ascending` as a side
  effect of being asked. Called per frame it would have reversed the serpentine
  ten times a second. It is now pure; `advanceSerpentine()` is the one place the
  direction changes.
- **`behind` had no dead band.** A dot a fifth of a degree the wrong side of the
  heading was reported as behind the operator, and the state flickered between
  `behind` and `advancing` on alternate frames for minutes.
- **`liftPromptDeg` (8°) was never used**, and when the rewrite reached for it
  as a magnitude gate it swallowed real asks — a sector needing 7.6° of lift
  reported `wantsLift: false`. There is now a separate 2° dead band, about the
  pose noise of a hand-held phone, which is all it is meant to reject.

## Why the panorama had a gap

Not for the reason the dot had problems, and not fixed by this change.

The stitcher matched **1096 pairs into 2 components, largest 114/115**. Then
`prune_outliers` dropped 537 of those pairs and the graph came apart into **5
components, largest 79**. The 36 excluded frames are one contiguous block,
azimuth 356° round to 299° — the last third of the lap, back toward the start,
where the loop-closure log already says the starting view could not be matched.

Frames 82 and 83 are 3° apart in bearing and 7° in elevation and *must* overlap;
83 was excluded anyway. So this is not a coverage hole. **The pruner is
shattering the graph**, and the sensor poses it prunes against had a median
residual of 4.7° with a p90 of 7.9° — against a guided-match radius of 112 px,
about 15°. Worth looking at next: prune should not be allowed to disconnect a
component that matched.

## Capture: 757 refusals against 115 photographs

The spacing gate was one fraction of the field of view everywhere, so a stretch
of horizon seen eight times cost exactly as many photographs as the band above
the roof nobody had seen once. It is now a ramp driven by `captureDemand()`,
which asks both maps what is still wanted at this exact pose:

- **0.10 of the field (90% overlap)** where either map wants something, or where
  the frame is aimed between bands — the connective tissue of a climb, and the
  last thing that should be refused.
- **0.30 of the field (70% overlap)** where both maps call the ground finished.
  Still far inside `MIN_PHOTO_OVERLAP`, so the chain cannot come apart.

`demand` is recorded in the capture audit, because "spacing-not-reached" alone
never said whether the app was refusing a frame it needed or one it did not.

## The survey now says how long it will take

At the start of pass 1, in the field log: how many minutes of walking, how many
minutes of building, and the instruction to stop the screen dimming *first* —
a phone that sleeps during a 16-minute build throws away the walk.

`js/survey-estimate.js` follows `js/build-progress.js`'s rule: measured, never
assumed. It is seeded from this capture (8.5 s of building per photograph, the
slower of the two available measurements, deliberately) and every figure is
replaced by this device's own after one full run. The estimate is quoted
coarsely — past ten minutes the difference between 16 and 17 is not information.

## Stale modules: fixed, not merely detected

Bumping VERSION to 0.21.0 produced a freshly reloaded page reading 0.20.2,
exactly as the 2026-08-21 handoff warned it would. `index.html` busted
`main.js?v=X` and nothing it imports.

There is now a generated **import map** in `index.html` mapping every module in
`js/` to a versioned URL, so a version bump busts all 31 files. Keys are
relative, so it survives a deployment sub-path.

**After changing VERSION, run `node tools/build-importmap.mjs`.**
`tests/import-map.test.mjs` fails if you forget, which is the only failure mode
that matters.

## Tools worth knowing about

| | |
|---|---|
| `node tools/replay-guidance.mjs <capture>/metadata/scan-coverage.json` | Replays a capture's recorded poses through the current dot logic and prints the before/after table above. The honest way to judge a change to `js/guidance.js`: a synthetic operator does what the test author imagined, a recorded one did what a person actually did. |
| `node tools/build-importmap.mjs` | Rewrites the import map. Run after every VERSION bump. |
| `node tests/dot-leads.test.mjs` | The field failure as a regression test: the ceiling, the band ask, the dot not walking behind the phone, and a tall column an obedient operator can actually finish. |

## First things to check next time

1. **Header version.** It should read v0.21.0 (2026-08-25). If the stale banner
   appears, the import map was not regenerated — force-reload before capturing.
2. **`aimSource` in the trail.** New field in `scan-coverage.json`. A session
   that is mostly `camera` is a dot instructing nobody; it should be mostly
   `band` with `lift`/`rest` at the edges of tall things.
3. **`demand` in `capture-audit.json`.** A `spacing-not-reached` at demand 0 is
   the policy working. At demand 1 it is not.
4. **`columnsBeyondReach` in `column-plan.json`.** Non-zero means the scene is
   genuinely taller than 60° and those tops are recorded as unmeasured — which
   is honest. It must never again mean a column that cannot complete.
5. Whether the pruner still disconnects a component that matched. That is the
   remaining cause of a hole in an otherwise good panorama.
