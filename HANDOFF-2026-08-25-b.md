# Horizon Scanner — v0.22.0, from the 22:23 back-yard capture

The second capture of 2026-08-25, on v0.21.0: a Pixel, 152 photographs, 146
seconds of walking. Two complaints, and they turn out to be one fault with two
faces.

---

## The dot lurched, and that is why the house ghosted

The reported symptom was "moving just a bit erratically, going from the top to
the bottom instantly". The recorded trail says exactly that, eighteen times:

```
 t=67.8   camera at 53.4°   ask jumps 55.9° -> 0.0°
 t=71.4   camera at  3.3°   ask jumps  0.0° -> 41.9°
 t=78.8   camera at 58.9°   ask jumps 59.5° -> 0.0°
```

`ColumnPlan.gapBand()` returned `ascending ? lowestGap : highestGap` — the far
end of the column, chosen by a flag, with no regard for where the camera was.
And the flag never flipped: `ascending` was still `true` in the archive at the
end of the survey. `advanceSerpentine()` only fires when a column completes *and*
its ring bin is covered, and most columns are released for other reasons, so
**every new column was entered at its bottom band from whatever height the
operator was already holding.**

The operator followed each lurch, which is where the second complaint comes from.
Chasing the dot from 55° to 0° and back produces fast tilts, and there was
**no tilt-rate gate at all** — `keyframeMotionAccepted` tested yaw and nothing
else, in an app whose entire capture pattern is vertical. So:

| | |
|---|---|
| frames taken while tilting >15°/s with yaw under 15°/s | **28 of 152** |
| fastest frame | **135°/s** combined |
| median `visualQuality`, frames over 15°/s | **0.240** |
| median `visualQuality`, frames under 8°/s | **0.437** |

A well-tracked frame on this scene scores about 0.25, so the fast ones are
sitting on the floor. That is motion blur, and it is the one defect no amount of
solving repairs: a smeared photograph yields fewer and worse SIFT features, its
pairs are the ones `prune_outliers` deletes, and a frame left holding one or two
weak pairs gets placed confidently in the wrong place. Rebuilt natively, this
capture pruned 587 of 1489 pairs and moved frames a **median of 17.0° from their
sensor poses, max 33.5°**.

That is what the ghost is. Look at the house crop: an entire translucent second
copy of the roofline and wall painted across the sky, offset by something like
15-20°. Not blur, not a small misregistration — a frame placed tens of degrees
out and then blended in. The sky has no texture for the seam cutter to hide a cut
in, so it routes the seam straight through the misplaced roofline.

**Measured ghosting: mean overlap disagreement 34.9, p95 97.7** — against 24.9
on the 20:03 capture. Over ~28 means visible doubling.

## What changed

**The vertical target is continuous now.** `gapBand` takes the camera's current
elevation and returns the nearest unfilled band **in the direction of travel**,
exhausting that direction before the sweep turns around. The direction itself is
read off where the camera is when a column is taken (`directionFrom`), not
carried in a flag that has to be flipped correctly at every hand-off. Arriving at
the top of one column, you come back down the next — which is what the serpentine
was always supposed to mean.

Three supporting pieces:

- **Band hysteresis.** Once a band is asked for it stays asked for until it
  fills. Choosing from the camera's position is what stops the lurch and would,
  alone, cause a twitch instead — a hand hovering on a boundary flipping the ask
  back and forth over a line the operator cannot see.
- **A vertical slew limit**, 30°/s. The horizontal target has had one since the
  beginning; the vertical had exponential smoothing and nothing else, which
  moves the dot 60% of the way to a new target in a single update.
- The one-band clamp against the camera, which was already there.

Replayed over the same 487 recorded samples:

| | field | now |
|---|---|---|
| worst single move of the drawn dot | **35.8°** | **9.2°** |
| p99 move of the drawn dot | 12.2° | 7.7° |
| moves over 20° | 2 | **0** |

In a 300-second simulated survey the dot reverses direction 29 times — about
once per column, which is the serpentine — and **never within 1.5 s of the
previous reversal**. No shivering.

**The motion gate is on both axes.** `keyframeMotionAccepted` now takes the tilt
rate and tests `hypot(yaw, tilt)` against 30°/s, down from 35 yaw-only. On this
capture that refuses 8 of 152 frames — 5% — and the spacing gate asks for a
replacement the moment the operator slows, so the cost is a fraction of a second
per refusal and the benefit is that no smeared photograph enters the graph.

**The app no longer photographs the ground.** The capture ended with seven frames
between -74° and -78°: the phone lowered while a pass-2 cleanup hold was still
running, which accepts on stillness and bearing and never looked at where the
camera was pointed. All seven were stranded by the solver and all seven produced
an alarming "photographs are not connected" warning telling the operator to go
and re-shoot a bearing. The floor is one whole frame below **the horizon** — not
below the sector's required elevation, which over a tall house is 40-60° and
would have refused the entire lower half of every tall column. On this capture
the floor refuses exactly those seven frames and nothing else; the lowest
legitimate frame was -7.2°.

## Frame density is not the cause — measured, controlled

The obvious suspect was the density increase shipped in v0.21.0 (0.10 of the
field where either map wants something, up from a flat 0.14). It took the frame
count from 115 to 152 and the doubly-covered fraction of the panel from 34% to
45%, and against a near object more overlap means more parallax to reconcile.

So it was tested directly. The same capture, the same solver, the same
everything, with the frame set thinned back to what the old 0.14 policy would
have kept:

| | 152 frames (0.10) | 125 frames (0.14) |
|---|---|---|
| painted | 68.2% | 68.1% |
| seen by 2+ frames | 45.2% | 45.2% |
| **mean overlap disagreement** | **34.9** | **36.0** |
| p95 disagreement | 97.7 | 100.3 |
| frames placed | 143 / 152 | 122 / 125 |
| frames moved from the sensors | 17.0° median | 20.3° median |

Denser is very slightly *better*, not worse. The painted and doubly-covered
fractions come out identical to a tenth of a percent, so this is not the metric
being computed over a smaller surface — it is the same panel, measured the same
way. Extra frames also constrain the solve better: the thinned set had to move
its frames 20.3° from the sensors against 17.0° for the full set.

**Keep the density.** The ghosting is misplaced frames, not crowded ones.

One thing this did confirm, twice over: the thinned set matched into **one
component containing all 125 frames**, and then `prune_outliers` shattered it
into four with three isolated. The full set went 3 components to 5 the same way.
The pruner is reliably disconnecting graphs that had matched — the same finding
as the 20:03 capture, now reproduced on independent data.

## What is still open

**The near house cannot be made ghost-free by a rotation-only model.** It is
close enough that walking your body around the phone leaves real parallax, and
the panorama has one rotation per frame to express it with. Note what the density
experiment above rules out: the answer is NOT to photograph it less. Extra
overlapping views of a near object do not compound the ghosting; they constrain
the solve better. The levers that remain are photographing it *sharply* — no
frames taken mid-swing — and standing as still as a person can while turning.

**Weakly-constrained frames are placed confidently — and one of them is the
ghost.** Measured on the solved output, datum-free: for each frame, compare its
angular separation from its eight nearest neighbours in the SOLVED set against
the same separations in the SENSOR set. Both are within one set, so the arbitrary
datum cancels and what is left is how much that frame's geometry was distorted.

Across all 152 frames the median distortion is **2.89°**. Frame **82** is
**28.87°** — a tenfold outlier, kept by the solver, placed in the main component,
and painted. That is the translucent house across the sky. Frames 65, 88 and 89
follow at 12-15°.

The motion association is real but partial: the fifteen worst frames have a
median combined angular rate of **15.6°/s** against **11.3°/s** for the rest. So
blur contributes, and the new 30°/s gate would have refused 3 of those 15 — it
helps, it is not the whole answer.

The whole answer is a check the stitcher does not currently make. A frame whose
solved geometry disagrees with its own sensors by ten times the median has not
been solved, it has been guessed, and painting it is worse than dropping it.
**This is not urgent for a field session**, because an archive can be re-stitched
any number of times without walking anywhere; capture changes are the ones that
have to be right before the walk.

The solver reports a median
residual of 0.111° after pruning, which sounds excellent and is measured only
over the matches that survived. A frame holding one or two pairs can sit 20° out
and still report a tiny residual. Worth building: refuse to paint a frame whose
solved pose disagrees with its sensor pose by more than a few times the sensor's
own scatter, or which is held by fewer than N verified pairs.

**The focal length is not stable.** The first solve converged on x0.7965 and the
post-prune solve on x0.9252 — a 16% swing on the same data. `k1` is imposed at
-0.10 and is degenerate with focal length. The handoff from 08-21 already names
this: solve k1 in `bundle_adjust`.

**Loop closure has failed on both captures.** "Could not match the starting
view." The gyro scale was also refused this run (measured 0.9684, too far from 1
to trust), so nothing absorbed it. A 3% scale error over a lap is ~11° of
accumulated azimuth, which is the same order as the 17° median pose correction.

## Reading the next capture

1. **Header must read v0.22.0 (2026-08-25).** After any VERSION change run
   `node tools/build-importmap.mjs`; `tests/import-map.test.mjs` fails if you
   forget.
2. `node tools/replay-guidance.mjs <capture>/metadata/scan-coverage.json` prints
   the before/after tables above, including the new VERTICAL CONTINUITY block.
   `HFOV=` and `VFOV=` override the assumed lens.
3. In `capture-audit.json`, `motion-too-fast` should now carry a `tiltRate`, and
   `below-survey-band` should be zero or near it.
4. In the trail, `aimSource` should be mostly `band`. Watch `dotElevationDeg`
   for steps over 3° — the slew limit makes that impossible at 10 Hz, so any
   would mean the limit is being bypassed.
5. Build the panorama and **wait for it**. The 22:23 debug ZIP was exported
   while the build was still running, so it contains `panoramaOptimization:
   null` and no stitch report — the archive predates the panorama it is supposed
   to explain. Export the ZIP after the build line appears in the log.
